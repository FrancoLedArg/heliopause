# NASA Space Apps 2026: Space Mission Design Game

Researched 2026-11-14 from the official challenge page (last edited 2026-11-13). Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/space-mission-design-game/

## The challenge

- **Difficulty:** Beginner/Youth, Intermediate. **Skill:** Games. One of 14 challenges in 2026.
- **Problem:** real mission design balances objectives, spacecraft, instruments, launch vehicles, budget, power, mass, communications and orbital constraints. Students rarely get to experience a mission "from concept to operation"; existing tools teach orbits, spacecraft or rockets in isolation.
- **Task:** an interactive game where players design, manage and simulate a complete space mission, making engineering decisions, managing limited resources, and seeing how each choice affects success.
- **Players could:** define an objective and requirements; design a spacecraft and select instruments; choose a launch vehicle and orbit; manage budget, mass, power, comms, data and lifetime.
- **Modes:** single-player or cooperative for up to 4 players.
- **A strong solution may include:** clear UI, mission configuration, resource management, multiplayer (up to 4), simulation, evaluation/scoring with feedback.
- **Audience:** students and aspiring engineers at any level of space knowledge.

## Potential considerations (optional, from NASA)

- **Game design:** accessible to all levels; single-player and team play with roles (mission planning, spacecraft engineering, science, comms, resource management); explain _why_ a mission succeeds or fails; let players modify and re-simulate; scoring; difficulty levels and scenarios.
- **Mission design:** start from the objective; every decision has consequences (more instruments → more mass, power, cost, data); rarely one perfect solution; realistic constraints.
- **Spacecraft and payload:** structure, power, batteries, comms, onboard computer, attitude control, instruments; each with mass, power, cost, data and science value; simple vs advanced components.
- **Orbit and launch:** several orbit types and their effect on coverage, comms and lifetime; launch vehicles differ in capacity, cost and reachable orbits; check mass/orbit compatibility with the vehicle.
- **Resources to display:** mass, power, budget, data, communications, time.
- **Collaboration:** roles, negotiating competing priorities, outcome depends on coordination.
- **Educational value:** plain-language explanations, tutorials, explain why, not just right/wrong.
- **Optional features:** random events, leaderboards, live resource dashboards, end-of-mission reports, save/share/compare designs, competitive mode, sandbox, post-mission analysis.

## Event, submission and judging

- **Event:** 14–15 Nov 2026. Submissions open Sat 14 Nov 09:00, close Sun 15 Nov 23:59 Local Event time.
- **Teams:** 1–6 members.
- **Demo:** a ≤30-second video **or** ≤7 slides, in English, at a public no-login link. Exceeding the limit disqualifies from Global Judging.
- **Final project:** a public link (website, repo, Drive or YouTube). A hosted working demo is not strictly required.
- **Judging (1–5 each):** Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance (meaningful NASA data integration), Presentation.
- **Timeline:** Global Nominees 3 Dec 2026; Finalists 17 Dec 2026; Winners 26 Jan 2027. "Best Mission Concept" is one of ten Global Awards.
